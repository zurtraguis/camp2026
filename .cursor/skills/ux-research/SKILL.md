# UX Research

Gather just-enough evidence to make confident product and design decisions, avoiding guesswork.

## When to Use This Skill

- Validating a problem or stakeholder need (internal or external)
- Choosing between design directions with evidence
- Defining success metrics and acceptance criteria from real needs
- Competitive/heuristic teardown of existing solutions

## Methods (pick by question, not by habit)

| Question | Method |
|----------|--------|
| "Why do users struggle?" | 5–7 user interviews |
| "How many feel X?" | Short survey (quant) |
| "Can they complete the task?" | Usability test (think-aloud) |
| "What do alternatives do?" | Competitive/heuristic teardown |
| "Where do they drop off?" | Analytics / journey review |

## Process

1. **Frame the decision** the research must inform (one sentence).
2. **Pick the lightest method** that answers it; timebox it.
3. **Write neutral questions** — no leading/loaded phrasing.
4. **Synthesize into findings** — patterns + supporting evidence, not anecdotes.
5. **Convert to actions** — each finding maps to a requirement, risk, or decision.

## Quality Bar

- [ ] Research tied to a real decision (not "nice to know")
- [ ] Sample and method stated; limitations acknowledged
- [ ] Findings are evidence-backed and prioritized
- [ ] Privacy/consent respected for any user data (LGPD/GDPR)

## Anti-patterns

- Leading questions that confirm a preexisting opinion
- Boiling one interview into a universal "truth"
- Research with no decision attached to it
